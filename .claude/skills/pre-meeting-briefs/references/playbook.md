# Brief Playbook — Adaptive Frameworks by Meeting Type × Deal Stage

This file is the strategic brain of the Pre-Meeting Brief skill. Claude reads this to determine:
- Which sections to emphasise or compress
- What talking point frameworks to apply
- What the desired outcome should be
- What the hidden angle might look like

Read the relevant section(s) for the meeting type + deal stage before drafting the brief.

---

## PLAYBOOK A — By Meeting Type

---

### A1. COLD_INTRO — First meeting, no prior relationship

**Brief emphasis:**
- Section 1 (Person) and Section 2 (Company) lead — invest the most depth here
- Section 3 (History) is minimal — note how the intro was arranged and by whom
- Section 5 (Pipeline) is speculative but important — what's the hypothesis for why we're meeting?
- Section 6 (Talking Points) is crucial — first impressions are set in 5 minutes

**Framing principle:** BugleRock should arrive knowing more about the counterparty than
they expect anyone to know. That asymmetry is the first impression.

**Talking point framework:**
1. **Open with genuine curiosity** — ask about something specific you found in your research.
   Never open with a pitch. ("I read that your firm recently expanded into infrastructure — I'd love
   to understand what's driving that.")
2. **Plant a relevance signal** — early in the conversation, establish why this meeting is
   timely for BugleRock specifically. Not a pitch, just a signal.
3. **Find the shared frame** — what is a market observation or challenge you both care about?
   Build rapport through shared perspective before any commercial framing.
4. **The soft probe** — one open question that helps reveal what they actually need and whether
   BugleRock can serve that need. ("What's the one thing you wish more advisors understood about
   how you think about X?")
5. **The path forward** — close with a natural next step that feels like a mutual decision,
   not a pitch close. ("Would it make sense to follow up with some specific material on how we've
   approached this?")

**What they likely want from this meeting:**
To assess whether BugleRock is worth their time. They are calibrating: credibility,
differentiation, and whether there is a specific reason to continue the conversation.

**Desired outcome:** A clear reason for a second meeting. Not a commitment to anything commercial.

**Hidden angle to probe:**
Who introduced you, and why? The introducer's motivation often reveals more about this
counterparty's actual needs than anything the counterparty will say in a first meeting.
Research the introducer too.

### A2. WARM_RECONNECT — Re-engaging after 60+ days

**Brief emphasis:**
- Section 3 (History) leads — what happened last time, and why did the relationship go quiet?
- Section 4 (Open Commitments) is critical — were there any unfulfilled commitments from before?
- Section 1/2 should check for what has changed about them since the last interaction

**Framing principle:** The gap needs to be acknowledged or bridged naturally. If BugleRock
went quiet (check email/Attio), own it briefly and move on. If they went quiet, don't
reference it — just pick up as if the relationship is ongoing.

**Talking point framework:**
1. **The bridge** — reference something specific from the last interaction to re-establish
   continuity. ("When we last spoke in [month], you mentioned X — I've been thinking about that.")
2. **The update** — what has changed at BugleRock that is relevant to them? New mandate,
   new capability, new deal that's analogous.
3. **What changed for them?** — probe what has changed in their world since you last spoke.
   Their circumstances may have evolved significantly.
4. **The re-qualification** — gently establish whether the original interest still exists,
   or whether the nature of the opportunity has changed.
5. **Rebook the relationship** — end with a specific cadence proposal. Relationships that
   go quiet twice rarely recover.

**Desired outcome:** A revived, scheduled cadence. Not necessarily a deal — just a
confirmed rhythm of contact.

### A3. DEAL_DEVELOP — Active deal, early to mid stage (Discovery → Proposal)

**Brief emphasis:**
- Section 5 (Pipeline) leads — what is the deal, where is it, what does this meeting need to do?
- Section 6 (Talking Points) is the most important section — this is where deals are built
- Section 4 (Open Commitments) is prominent — fulfilling commitments is how deals advance

**Framing principle:** At this stage, BugleRock is simultaneously building trust, demonstrating
capability, and qualifying the counterparty. Every conversation must advance the deal by
at least one concrete step.

**Talking point framework by sub-stage:**

*Stage 1 — DISCOVERY:*
1. **Understand before proposing** — this meeting's job is to understand their decision
   criteria, not to pitch. ("Help me understand how you'd evaluate a partner on this.")
2. **Qualify the opportunity** — what is the actual size, timing, and decision path?
3. **Establish the pain** — what is not working about how they've approached this before?
4. **Plant a capability signal** — mention one specific thing BugleRock has done that is
   relevant, without a full pitch. Credibility by example.
5. **Agree the next step explicitly** — "Based on what we've discussed, the most useful thing
   I could bring to the next conversation would be X. Does that make sense?"

*Stage 2 — QUALIFIED:*
1. **Confirm the fit** — restate your understanding of their need and check it's accurate.
2. **Introduce the BugleRock angle** — now is the time to frame the specific product,
   strategy, or service that maps to their need. One option, not a menu.
3. **Surface the objections early** — "What would need to be true for this to work for you?"
4. **Discuss process** — who else needs to be involved? What does their decision process look like?
5. **Propose the IC note or next formal step** — "The most natural next step would be X. When
   can I send that through?"

*Stage 3 — PROPOSAL:*
1. **Check if they've read the proposal** — if not, offer to walk through it. If yes, go
   straight to questions and objections.
2. **Address the top objection first** — from your research and Attio notes, what is their
   most likely concern? Name it before they do.
3. **Restate the value in their language** — mirror back the problem they described and
   show how the proposal solves it.
4. **The commercial ask** — have a specific question about the fee or terms that tests
   their readiness to progress.
5. **Timeline alignment** — what is their timing? Create a shared urgency without pressure.

**Hidden angle at DEAL_DEVELOP stage:**
Who else in their organisation needs to be bought in? The person you're meeting is rarely
the sole decision-maker. Find the other stakeholders and start mapping a path to them.

**Desired outcome:** Advancement by exactly one deal stage. Not two. One.

### A4. DEAL_ADVANCE — Term sheet, DD, IC committee stage

**Brief emphasis:**
- Section 5 (Pipeline) and Section 6 (Talking Points) dominate
- Section 2 (Company) expands to include financial metrics and deal context
- Section 7 (What They'll Ask) becomes critical — anticipate every objection
- Section 9 (Desired Outcome) must be surgical — name the specific commercial milestone

**Framing principle:** At this stage, deals are won or lost on execution confidence and
relationship trust. The counterparty is already interested — they need to feel that
BugleRock can deliver, and that the terms are fair. This is not a discovery conversation.

**Talking point framework:**
1. **Confirm the headline terms** — start by aligning on the key commercial terms you
   believe are agreed. Avoid ambiguity accumulating.
2. **The open point** — identify the one remaining unresolved issue and address it directly.
   Leaving open points to drift is how deals die.
3. **Execution confidence** — share one specific example of how BugleRock has delivered
   in a comparable situation. Evidence, not assertion.
4. **The timeline** — propose a specific closing timeline. Urgency with a rationale
   ("We have two other conversations that will complete before month-end") is legitimate.
5. **The silent concern** — every deal at this stage has a concern the counterparty hasn't
   voiced. Invite it: "Is there anything I haven't addressed that you're still thinking about?"

**Anticipated questions to prepare for:**
- Fees / economics
- Track record and references
- What happens if things go wrong
- Exit or termination clauses
- Who specifically will handle their account

**Desired outcome:** Agreement on a specific next milestone — IC approval, DD call date,
term sheet sign-off, mandate letter drafting commenced.

### A5. DEAL_CLOSE — Final negotiation or signing

**Brief emphasis:**
- Strip all background — they know who BugleRock is
- Lead with: what terms are still open? What is the latest position on each?
- Section 4 (Commitments) lists every outstanding item needed to close
- Section 9 (Outcome) must be: sign today, or get a date certain for signing

**Framing principle:** Remove friction. Every open point is a potential deal-kill at this
stage. BugleRock's job is to close the gap on each open point, not to re-pitch.

**Talking point framework:**
1. **Enumerate the open points** — "I want to make sure we address everything today.
   As I understand it, the remaining open points are X, Y, and Z. Is that your list too?"
2. **Close each point in order of ease** — start with what you can resolve easily to
   build momentum.
3. **The hold-out point** — identify which open point is the real sticking point. The others
   are often proxies for one underlying concern. Name it.
4. **The bridging offer** — prepare a specific concession or creative structure that could
   break the sticking point without materially changing the deal economics.
5. **The close** — "If we can agree on [the sticking point] today, is there anything else
   that would prevent us from moving forward?" Then stop talking.

**Desired outcome:** Signed agreement, or a date certain with a written summary of agreed terms.

### A6. RELATIONSHIP_BUILD — Strategic relationship nurturing

**Brief emphasis:**
- Section 1 (Person) leads — invest in knowing what has changed for them since you last met
- Section 3 (History) reminds the invoker of the relationship arc
- Section 6 (Talking Points) is conversational, not commercial

**Framing principle:** These meetings are investments in future optionality. The job is
to deepen trust and demonstrate consistent value — not to pitch. The commercial return
comes later and often indirectly.

**Talking point framework:**
1. **What's changed for them?** — lead with genuine curiosity about their world.
2. **Share one relevant insight** — something from the market, a deal you've seen, an
   observation on their sector. Give value before asking for anything.
3. **The quiet ask** — one light, low-pressure commercial thread: "We've been working on X —
   I'd love to share the thinking sometime if it's useful." Leave it open.
4. **The referral probe** — "Is there anyone in your network we should know who's thinking
   about [relevant topic]?" Referral asks work when the relationship is warm.
5. **Reconfirm the cadence** — always leave with a natural next touchpoint agreed.

**Desired outcome:** Relationship depth maintained or deepened. One actionable output
(a referral, an introduction, a piece of shared intelligence).

### A7. INVESTOR_LP — LP or capital-raising conversation

**Brief emphasis:**
- Section 2 (Company) expands significantly — LP diligence is deep; they will have done research
- Section 5 (Pipeline) focuses on fund strategy and performance context
- Section 6 (Talking Points) is tailored to LP concerns: governance, risk management, liquidity, reporting

**Framing principle:** LPs are evaluating a long-term relationship, not a transaction.
They are assessing people as much as performance. Governance, process, and alignment
matter as much as returns.

**LP-specific talking points:**
1. **Investment thesis clarity** — state the strategy and edge in 2 sentences. If you can't,
   neither can they defend investing in you.
2. **Risk framework** — how does the fund think about downside? LPs hate surprises more than
   they hate losses.
3. **Portfolio construction rationale** — why these allocations, at these weights, with this
   liquidity profile?
4. **Team and governance** — who makes decisions, how, and what happens if a key person leaves?
5. **The alignment question** — how is the GP eating their own cooking? Co-investment,
   deferred carry, GP commitment size.

**Anticipated LP concerns:**
- Track record (especially if early-stage fund)
- Fee structure vs. industry standard
- Reporting frequency and transparency
- Key-person risk
- Exit and liquidity mechanisms

**Desired outcome:** Clear path to a term sheet / subscription agreement, or a specific
follow-up action (reference call, site visit, further DD request).

---

### A8. CO_INVESTOR — Co-investment or syndication

**Brief emphasis:**
- Section 5 (Pipeline) leads — which deal, what structure, what role for each party?
- Section 6 (Talking Points) focuses on alignment of interests and economics
- Section 2 (Company) covers the underlying target company if that's the subject

**Key considerations:**
- What is the proposed economic split? What is BugleRock's basis for its position?
- Are there any other parties in the syndicate? Is there a lead?
- What are the governance rights at stake — board seat, information rights, veto?
- What is the timeline for close?

**Desired outcome:** Confirmed co-investment terms or a clear next step in the syndication process.

---

### A9. INTRO_REFERRAL — Introduction via a mutual contact

**Brief emphasis:**
- Research the mutual contact as well as the new counterparty — understand *why* this
  introduction was made and what the referrer's interest is
- Section 1 (Person) and Section 2 (Company) lead
- Section 5 (Pipeline) is speculative but must be present

**Framing principle:** The referrer's credibility is on the line, and so is yours.
Arrive prepared well enough to justify the introduction.

**Desired outcome:** Confirm whether there is a genuine opportunity and agree a follow-up
with a specific agenda.

## PLAYBOOK B — Deal Stage Modifiers

These apply on top of the meeting type framework above when a deal stage is identified.

| Stage | Key modifier |
|---|---|
| FIRST_CONTACT | Research is the primary input; Section 1+2 are the most important sections |
| DISCOVERY | Understanding > pitching; talking points are questions, not statements |
| QUALIFIED | Start proposing; introduce one specific BugleRock option |
| PROPOSAL | Combat objections; confirm process; close the next step |
| NEGOTIATION | Every point is commercial; have a bridging position ready |
| DUE_DILIGENCE | Provide evidence; anticipate data requests; manage timeline |
| CLOSING | Strip everything except the open points; prepare a concession |
| LIVE_MANDATE | Execution focus; relationship maintenance; upsell/cross-sell readiness |
| POST_DEAL | Referral harvest; relationship reinvestment; case study potential |

## PLAYBOOK C — The Hidden Angle Framework

For every meeting, regardless of type or stage, force one non-obvious angle into Section 5.
Use these prompts to find it:

**The network lens:**
Who does this person know that BugleRock has been trying to reach? Is there a warm path
through them to someone more valuable? Can you create that introduction?

**The timing lens:**
What is happening in their company or sector RIGHT NOW that creates an unusual opening?
A recent fundraise creates capital deployment pressure. A leadership change creates mandate
uncertainty. A regulatory event creates advisory need. Scan Section 2B news carefully.

**The competitive lens:**
Are they also talking to a BugleRock competitor? What does BugleRock offer that the
competitor cannot? What does the competitor offer that BugleRock doesn't — and is that
a gap worth addressing?

**The lateral lens:**
This meeting is nominally about X. But what is the second-order opportunity?
- A company founder pitching a deal is also a potential investor in BugleRock's fund
- A family office considering a mandate is also a potential co-investor in deals
- A PE firm's CFO is also a potential future hire — or a source for talent intelligence
- A banker from another firm is also a potential joint-mandate or referral partner

**The constraint lens:**
What constraint is the counterparty operating under that BugleRock could relieve?
Timeline pressure, capital constraints, geographic limitations, regulatory hurdles —
any of these is an opportunity to provide value and deepen the relationship.

**The information asymmetry lens:**
What does BugleRock know that the counterparty doesn't — about their sector, their
competitive position, a relevant deal in the market? Sharing this selectively and
appropriately is one of the highest-value things a trusted advisor can do.

| COLD_INTRO | Clear reason for Meeting 2 | Confirmed follow-up email | "I'll send you X — does [date] work for a follow-up?" |
| WARM_RECONNECT | Revived relationship + confirmed next touchpoint | Mutual agreement to re-engage | Book a specific next meeting before leaving |
| DEAL_DEVELOP / Discovery | Advance to Qualified stage | Confirm their decision criteria | "Can I send the IC note / proposal by [date]?" |
| DEAL_DEVELOP / Proposal | Advance to Negotiation | Address top objection, book follow-up | "Can we agree a call to walk through your questions?" |
| DEAL_ADVANCE | Agree on specific next milestone | Remove one major open point | "I'll send the revised [term sheet / doc] by [date]" |
| DEAL_CLOSE | Signed agreement | Date certain for signing | "Let's confirm [date] as the signing date" |
| RELATIONSHIP_BUILD | Referral or warm intro | Shared insight exchanged | "I'll send you that research / connect you with X" |
| INVESTOR_LP | Term sheet / subscription or specific follow-up | Reference call scheduled | "Can I send the fund docs / DDQ by [date]?" |
| CO_INVESTOR | Agreed co-invest economics | MOU or term sheet agreed in principle | "I'll circulate a draft co-invest term sheet" |
| INTRO_REFERRAL | Confirm genuine opportunity | Agree scope of next conversation | "Let's follow up with a more specific agenda" |
