# The Angle Playbook

The Angle is the single most important output of the synthesis step.
It is the one insight or positioning that, if surfaced well, shifts the meeting in your favour.

A brief without an angle is just a research summary. A brief with a sharp angle
is a competitive weapon.

---

## What makes a good Angle

**Specific:** References this company, this person, this moment. Never generic.
**Actionable:** Can be surfaced in the first 10 minutes of the meeting.
**Asymmetric:** You know something (or see something) they don't expect you to know.
**Serves you:** It advances BugleRock's commercial or relationship objective.

**Test:** Read the angle out loud. Would a stranger understand immediately why it matters?
If not, it's too vague. Sharpen it.

---

## How to find the Angle

Work through this checklist during Step 4B synthesis. The first strong signal wins.

### Signal 1 — The public statement match

Scan the attendee's recent public statements (LinkedIn, Twitter/X, interviews, podcasts).
Look for: a problem they've named, a frustration they've expressed, a goal they've stated.

If found:
→ The angle is: "They said [X]. We have [Y]. Surface [Y] as a direct response to [X]."
→ Opening line: "I saw your [post/interview] about [X] — that's exactly the problem we
  designed our [approach] to solve."

### Signal 2 — The timing event

Check recent news (< 90 days) for the company or person.
Look for: a fundraise, an acquisition, a new hire, a regulatory event, a competitor move,
a public announcement.

If found:
→ The angle is: "Something just happened that creates a specific opportunity or pressure
  for them. We can be the response to it."
→ Opening line: "Congratulations on [event]. Given what that typically triggers in your
  sector, I imagine [X] is now on the agenda — is that right?"

### Signal 3 — The gap in their story

Compare their current situation (from Traxcn/Drive + web) against where similar companies
are at their stage. Look for: an absent advisor on an active process, an underfunded round,
a stalled mandate, a missing structural piece.

If found:
→ The angle is: "There's a gap that they may or may not have seen. Naming it first = credibility."
→ Opening line: "From what I can see, you've done [X and Y] really well — the one thing
  I don't see is [Z], and in most comparable situations that's where friction shows up."

### Signal 4 — The network connection

Check your own email, Attio, and Drive for: a reference client in the same sector,
a co-investor they'd value, a contact who can validate BugleRock, an intro that is useful to them.

If found:
→ The angle is: "You have something they need. Offer it."
→ Opening line: "Before we get into anything else — [Mutual contact] mentioned you're
  exploring [X]. I know [person/company] who went through the same thing recently.
  I'd be happy to connect you."

### Signal 5 — The contrarian view

Look at what everyone is telling them (inferred from sector news + their public statements).
Is there a well-reasoned counter-view that the data supports?

If found:
→ The angle is: "Challenge their assumptions with data. Make them think."
→ Opening line: "I know the consensus view is [X]. I actually think that's wrong for your
  specific situation — here's what the data shows."

### Signal 6 — The comparable mandate

Check Drive and Attio for: a past BugleRock mandate in the same sector, stage, or geography.
If a close comparable exists, it removes perceived risk and builds credibility instantly.

If found:
→ The angle is: "We've done this before. Show them."
→ Opening line: "We ran an almost identical process for [X] in [year]. I won't name names
  without permission, but happy to walk you through what we learned and how we'd apply it here."

## Angle quality levels

**Level 1 (weak):** "Establish credibility and understand their needs."
→ Not an angle. This is the default approach. Any advisor shows up this way.

**Level 2 (acceptable):** "They're in Series C, which typically triggers a need for
  structured liquidity options for early investors."
→ This is a sector-level angle. Better than nothing, but not specific to this person.

**Level 3 (strong):** "Their CEO just posted about being 'stuck between two term sheets
  with incompatible structures' — that's exactly the cross-border structuring problem we
  solved for [Comp X]. Surfacing the comp immediately shows we understand the problem."
→ This is a person-specific, moment-specific angle. This wins meetings.

**Level 4 (exceptional):** Level 3 + a network connection or concrete proof point that
  removes the risk of working with BugleRock.
→ "Same angle as Level 3, AND we have a reference from [Comp X] who will take a call."

## Stale data constraint on angle selection

When the primary data sources for this meeting are stale — specifically, when the most recent
Drive document or Traxcn report is older than 9 months AND web validation could not confirm
the key facts — apply this constraint before selecting an angle:

**Only use a named angle archetype if it is grounded in high-confidence, recent data.**
High-confidence means: ✅ Verified from a source dated within the last 6 months, OR
💬 User-provided in this session.

**If the only available signal for an angle is stale and unvalidated:**
→ Do not use it as the angle. A stale Mirror Angle or Timing Angle based on outdated
  information will make the banker look uninformed, not credible.
→ Drop down to Discovery mode instead. Declare:
  > `THE ANGLE: No high-confidence angle — primary data is stale (> 9 months, unvalidated).`
  > `Approach: Discovery mode — verify their current situation before making any claims.`

**Signals that CAN still anchor an angle despite stale company data:**
- A recent public statement by the attendee (< 90 days) → Mirror Angle still valid
- A recent news event about the company (< 90 days from web search) → Timing Angle still valid
- A verified network connection from your own email/Attio → Network Angle still valid
- A comparable BugleRock mandate (Drive document date is irrelevant here — the mandate happened) → Social Proof Angle still valid

**In short:** stale company financials do not invalidate all angles — only the specific
claims that depend on those financials. Isolate what is fresh vs. what is stale and
build the angle on fresh signals only.

---

## When no strong angle exists

If all signals are weak or missing (true cold call, very limited public data, no comparable
mandate), use the Discovery angle:

**The Discovery Angle:** "I'm here to understand your world, not to pitch.
I've done enough research to ask the right questions. Let's see if there's a fit."

→ In the brief: the angle becomes the key question to ask, not a statement to make.
→ Opening line: "I've been doing some work in your sector and I have a hypothesis
  about [X]. But I'd rather hear your view first — am I close?"

This is not a failure mode. A well-run discovery meeting is just as valuable as a pitch.
It just requires different preparation.

---

## The Angle in the brief

State the Angle in:
1. The Quick Situational Strip (header)
2. Section 5 (Deal & Opportunity Map)
3. Talking Point #1 (as the opening move)

In the email delivery: state the angle in one sentence in the email body.
The person reading the brief should be able to recite the angle from memory
before walking into the room.
